# Biodiversity Informatics

**Biodiversity Informatics** is an open, research-driven book project about how biodiversity knowledge becomes data.

The project explores the concepts, infrastructures, standards, software systems, and unresolved problems involved in representing biodiversity computationally. It combines conceptual explanation with real-world examples, datasets, technical demonstrations, and small research exercises.

## Work in progress

This project is at an early stage and is very much a **work in progress**.

The chapters are being developed iteratively through research, writing, experimentation, and practical work with biodiversity data and collection-management systems. The structure, terminology, examples, and conclusions may change substantially as the project develops.

The repository should therefore be understood as a public research and learning project rather than a finished or authoritative textbook.

Corrections, suggestions, references, and discussion are welcome.

## Approach

The project is built around a simple idea: biodiversity informatics is best understood through the problems it is trying to solve.

Rather than treating standards, databases, and software systems as isolated topics, the book asks questions such as:

* What exactly is biodiversity information?
* How does a physical specimen become a digital record?
* What is the relationship between an organism, specimen, occurrence, collecting event, and taxon?
* How do different systems determine that two records refer to the same thing?
* What happens when rich institutional data are transformed into standardized formats?
* How should uncertainty, provenance, historical information, and changing scientific knowledge be represented?
* What problems remain unresolved in current biodiversity information infrastructures?

Where possible, chapters will move through:

**concept → problem → real example → implementation or experiment → limitations → open questions**

## Planned topics

The eventual scope may include:

* biodiversity information and its history
* specimens, observations, occurrences, and collecting events
* taxonomy and taxonomic change
* identifiers and persistent identity
* collections-management systems
* spreadsheets, legacy data, and data migration
* Darwin Core and other biodiversity data standards
* data integration and interoperability
* people, places, and entity reconciliation
* georeferencing and geographic uncertainty
* data quality and provenance
* aggregators and global biodiversity infrastructures
* digital specimens and DiSSCo
* linked data and knowledge graphs
* computational methods and AI
* unresolved conceptual and technical problems in biodiversity informatics

The table of contents will evolve as the research develops.

## Chapters

The first chapter asks a foundational question:

### 1. What is biodiversity information?

It begins with the apparently simple case of a natural-history specimen and examines how that physical object becomes represented through cataloguing, digitization, taxonomy, databases, standards, and larger biodiversity infrastructures.

Additional chapters will be added incrementally.

## Examples and computational work

The project is intended to include more than explanatory prose.

Where useful, chapters will be accompanied by:

* real or realistic biodiversity datasets
* data-model comparisons
* mapping and reconciliation exercises
* diagrams
* scripts and notebooks
* API examples
* data-quality experiments
* worked examples using biodiversity standards
* case studies involving collection-management and aggregation systems

A guiding principle of the project is that major concepts should, wherever possible, be connected to something concrete that can be inspected, tested, or reproduced.

## Open and reproducible publishing

The book is being developed openly on GitHub.

The longer-term goal is to produce several outputs from the same source material, potentially including:

* an online open-access book
* PDF and EPUB editions
* a print edition
* computational labs and datasets
* machine-readable chapter material
* AI-oriented study and research companions

The publishing infrastructure will be developed gradually as the manuscript grows.

## Status
